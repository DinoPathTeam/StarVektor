# Publisher agreement review

A record of what the license agreements (EULAs) of the publishers whose games might be used to **evaluate** StarVektor say. It is the team's reading, not legal advice. Texts can change and vary by region and edition: before relying on one, the current version is re-read.

> **Early stage.** This record grows as publishers are reviewed. Anything not listed here has not been reviewed.

**Last review:** 2026-11-01.

## How to read this

- No agreement read mentions capture or frame generation. Only Epic's mentions AI (training of generative AI, see below). Silence neither allows nor forbids explicitly.
- What each agreement regulates are **acts**; intent does not count. That is why the project's defense is to describe precisely what it does and does not do, see [usage-statement.md](usage-statement.md).
- Statuses: **No clause against** (per our reading), **Broad clauses** (assessed with caution and, ideally, written permission), **Excluded for now** (not used), **Unverified**.

## Anti-cheat

The main risk in online games is operational rather than contractual: an anti-cheat can treat an injected layer as an intrusion. Valve's documentation warns that VAC can produce false positives with overlays and recording software. That is why the scope is single-player and offline (D9). The compatibility status of each anti-cheat on Linux changes, and is checked per title before testing.

### Check of the candidate titles (2026-10-31)

The candidates listed in `data/sources.toml` (`evaluation_candidates`) were compared against three sources: ProtonDB reports (first page of reports per title), the GamingOnLinux anti-cheat list (285 rows) and the Are We Anti-Cheat Yet database (1,167 games). None of the candidates appears in the last two, and no ProtonDB report mentions an anti-cheat. The only protection mentioned is Denuvo (anti-tamper) in Maneater, in a ProtonDB report.

Resident Evil 4 (2023 remake) was added later on 2026-10-31 and checked the same way: ProtonDB Gold and Deck Verified with no anti-cheat mention in the first page of reports, and no entry in either list (the Are We Anti-Cheat Yet matches are other Resident Evil games).

Metro 2033 Redux was promoted to the evaluation set after its Steam store page was read on 2026-10-31: publisher Deep Silver, single-player, Linux support, and no anti-cheat or third-party DRM listed on the page.

Euro Truck Simulator 2 was added on 2026-11-01 and checked the same way. Its Steam store page lists single-player, online co-op (Convoy), Steam Workshop and Steam Cloud, and no anti-cheat or third-party DRM; the optional World of Trucks service needs no registration to play. It has no entry in the Are We Anti-Cheat Yet database (1,167 games, none matching Euro Truck Simulator or American Truck Simulator), and the first page of ProtonDB reports (40 of 504) mentions no anti-cheat. One report mentions TruckersMP, a third-party multiplayer client; StarVektor is used only in the single-player game, never in Convoy, TruckersMP or World of Trucks, and without Workshop or third-party mods. The GamingOnLinux list was not re-read for this title. ProtonDB reports a native Linux build (OpenGL) and a Windows build under Proton: the build used is recorded with each measurement.

**Limits.** This is absence of evidence, not proof. Both lists track anti-cheats of games with online modes, ProtonDB reports are user opinions, and only the first page of reports was read. Each title is still checked on its own store page before a capture is made.
